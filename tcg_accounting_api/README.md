# TCG accounting

## Business model

1. Buy mixed lots of second-hand trading cards on Facebook Marketplace or Trade
   Me. Around NZ$30 per 1,000 cards is a buying benchmark. The price and size
   vary by lot.
2. Sort the cards using roughly 25 cents per card as the cutoff. List the
   higher-value singles on FetchTCG and sell the remaining cards in bulk on
   Trade Me.
3. Charge buyers for cards and any applicable shipping. FetchTCG deducts seller
   fees and credits the proceeds to a wallet. Withdrawals go to the dedicated
   business bank account. Buy outbound postage through Trade Me Book a Courier.

The business operates in New Zealand as a sole trader and is currently
unregistered for GST. Report the net business result on the personal **IR3** for
the tax year from **1 April to 31 March**. Include any GST paid as part of business
costs while unregistered. [Inland Revenue's online trading guide][online-trading]
and [schedule of business income (IR3B)][ir3b].

## Income tax

**Business profit for tax = gross sales − cost of goods sold − operating expenses
− equipment deductions.**

### Sales, fees and shipping

Record the gross amount earned from Trade Me and FetchTCG, including shipping
charged to buyers, before seller fees. For a $20 card order with $5 buyer-paid
shipping and a $1 seller fee, record $25 in revenue and $1 in fees. Record the
actual outbound courier charge as a selling expense. A Fetch wallet withdrawal
is a transfer to the bank. At 31 March, include sales where the right to payment
has arisen, including funds available in Fetch. Check pending orders around
year-end against the payment terms. [Inland Revenue's trading stock income
ruling][income-timing] and [FetchTCG's wallet guide][fetch-wallet].

### Stock purchases and the $10,000 rule

Record each lot at its actual purchase cost, including shipping to get the cards
to the business. If the seller quotes one delivered price, record that amount.
For example, 10,000 cards for $200 delivered and 2,000 cards for $75 delivered
give $275 in stock purchases. Add separately paid inbound freight to the lot
cost. [Inland Revenue's stock valuation guide][stock-valuation].

**Test the shortcut each year at 31 March.** Reasonably estimate the value of all
unsold stock, including listed singles, unsold bulk, unsorted lots and cards
bought in earlier years. Include those earlier purchases even if last year's
closing stock for tax was $0. At cost, use the purchase cost including inbound
shipping. You can use cost even if the Fetch asking prices total more than
$10,000. If all your stock came from $2,200 of delivered purchases, $2,200 is an
upper bound on the cost of the cards still on hand. [Inland Revenue's stock
valuation guide][stock-valuation].

If annual turnover is **$1.3 million or less** and stock on hand is reasonably
estimated at **under $10,000**, you can use the opening tax stock value as the
closing tax stock value. The two figures cancel, so the stock deduction equals
that year's purchases. The threshold applies to stock still on hand. Annual
purchases can exceed $10,000 and still qualify. At exactly $10,000 of closing
stock, use an ordinary valuation method. [Inland Revenue's financial statements
guide (IR10G)][ir10g].

#### Crossing the threshold

Start with no stock, buy $5,000 of cards in year one and use the shortcut. Deduct
$5,000 and carry forward $0 as the year-two opening tax value. Buy another $7,000
in year two. Assuming turnover still qualifies, the year-two result depends on
what is on hand at 31 March.

- **$9,000 of cards at cost.** Use the shortcut again and deduct the full $7,000
  of new purchases. The closing tax stock value is $0.
- **All $12,000 of cards still on hand at cost.** Use the ordinary calculation.
  Opening tax stock of $0 plus $7,000 in purchases minus $12,000 in closing stock
  gives **−$5,000 in cost of goods sold**, which increases taxable profit by
  $5,000. This effectively reverses the year-one deduction. The $12,000 carries
  forward as the year-three opening tax value. [Inland Revenue's trading stock
  guidance][trading-stock] and [financial statements guide (IR10G)][ir10g].

#### How the shortcut affects deduction timing

For example, start with no stock, buy $2,200 of cards, and hold $1,500 of those
cards at cost on 31 March. Assume gross sales of $1,600 and other deductible
expenses of $600 in year one. In year two, sell the remaining cards, buy no new
stock, and earn $2,000 after operating expenses.

| Tax calculation                       | Use the $10,000 rule in year one | Ordinary stock calculation |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------: | -------------------------: |
| Year one closing stock for tax        |                               $0 |                     $1,500 |
| Year one stock deduction              |                           $2,200 |                       $700 |
| Year one business result              |                      $1,200 loss |                $300 profit |
| Year two stock deduction              |                               $0 |                     $1,500 |
| Year two business result              |                    $2,000 profit |                $500 profit |
| Total business result over both years |                      $800 profit |                $800 profit |

The rule brings the $1,500 deduction forward to year one in this example.

### Tracking stock as it grows

1. **Log each purchase lot.** Record a batch ID, date, seller, approximate card
   count and actual delivered cost. When sorting a mixed lot, record how you
   allocate its cost between bulk and singles. Document the basis for material
   splits. Relative resale values are one possible basis to check before relying
   on it for tax.
2. **Use an annual weighted average for comparable cards.** Pool comparable
   bulk from differently priced lots and track materially different singles
   separately. For each comparable group, divide **opening inventory cost plus
   this year's added cost** by **opening quantity plus this year's added
   quantity**. [NZ IAS 2, paragraphs 23–27][nz-ias-2] and [Inland Revenue's stock
   valuation guide][stock-valuation].
3. **Count what remains at 31 March.** Multiply each group's remaining quantity
   by its unrounded average cost. Carry forward the quantity and underlying cost
   in the stock worksheet, even when the shortcut gives a different closing
   value for the tax return. Recalculate the average each year. [NZ IAS 2,
   paragraph 27][nz-ias-2] and [Inland Revenue's trading stock
   guidance][trading-stock].

For example, 4,000 comparable bulk cards costing $80 at the start of the year
plus 2,000 added cards costing $80 gives an average of **$160 ÷ 6,000**, or about
**2.67 cents per card**. If 3,000 cards remain, carry them forward at **$80** using
the unrounded rate.

Calculate annual cost of goods sold as **opening tax stock + purchases − closing
tax stock**. The stock totals give you the full annual calculation. Keep the
marketplace order records as evidence of revenue. [Inland Revenue's financial
statements guide (IR10G)][ir10g].

### Operating expenses and equipment

- Deduct the business share of courier charges, mailers, sleeves, top loaders,
  storage rental and marketplace fees. [Inland Revenue's business expenses
  guide][business-expenses].
- A qualifying low-value asset costing **$1,000 or less** can generally be
  deducted in full in the year it is bought and available for business use. The
  $150 standalone thermal printer is an example. The threshold includes GST
  while the business is unregistered. [Inland Revenue's depreciation
  guidance][depreciation].
- A **$2,000 scanner** used entirely for the business would generally be
  depreciated. If it qualifies for **Investment Boost**, such as an eligible new
  scanner first available for business use on or after 22 May 2025, claim **$400
  upfront** and depreciate the remaining **$1,600**. A scanner already used in New
  Zealand generally gets ordinary depreciation. [Inland Revenue's Investment
  Boost guidance][investment-boost].

## GST registration at $60,000

Track gross taxable sales from both marketplaces, including shipping charged to
buyers, before deducting fees and expenses. Add turnover from any other taxable
business activity carried on by the same sole trader. Registration is generally
required if turnover **exceeds $60,000** over a rolling 12-month period or is
reasonably expected to exceed that amount in the next 12 months. [Inland
Revenue's GST registration guidance][gst-registration] and [GST Act, section
51][gst-act].

## Records to keep

Keep the bank statements and the records that explain each payment or deposit.
Readable digital records are fine. Save your own copies and retain them for
**at least seven tax years**. [Inland Revenue's record-keeping guidance][records].

| Activity                     | Evidence                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| FetchTCG sales               | Order records or a detailed export showing the sale date, goods, buyer, prices, shipping and refunds. Keep wallet activity for fees and payouts and match the payouts to the bank.               |
| Trade Me sales and postage   | Sold items export, plus records for shipping or refunds agreed separately. Save the account invoices covering seller fees and Book a Courier charges.                                            |
| Bulk purchases               | A listing, order confirmation, receipt or message thread showing the lot, seller, date and amount, plus proof of payment. For cash purchases, ask for a receipt or a message confirming payment. |
| Other expenses and equipment | Itemised receipts or invoices. For Investment Boost, also keep evidence of when the asset was first available for use.                                                                           |
| Closing stock                | The 31 March estimate, stock worksheet and supporting purchase records. Keep the quantities and underlying costs alongside the values used for tax.                                              |

Export Trade Me sales monthly to stay within its 45-day self-service export
window. [Trade Me's sold items export guide][trademe-sales], [Trade Me's account
invoice guide][trademe-invoices] and [FetchTCG's sales export guide][fetch-sales].
